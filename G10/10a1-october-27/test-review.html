Alexandra Tamworth: Poet

Alexandra Tamworth is one of the region’s best-loved poets. She’s been writing for more than 15 years, and has produced four books of poems for teenagers. She is, she explains, very aware that poetry is sometimes seen as a bit old-fashioned. She is determined to use her fame to help her demonstrate that this is wrong, and to help young writers. One way she aims to do so is by encouraging people to write about topics that really matter to younger generations. This isn’t to say that there’s no place for traditional poetry, of course. But Alexandra is very keen to point out how vital it is to be able to grab your reader’s attention from the start. That, she says, is key.

And as well as writing her own poems, she is keen to prove poetry-writing is something anyone can do, and she has a particular interest in encouraging young people to have a go. She runs poetry sessions in libraries for young writers, which include various techniques to help them get started. A situation that she understands can be a potential issue, particularly for first-time writers, is simply knowing where to begin! One idea that often proves productive, she says, is to look at the similarity between poems and lyrics. This helps people to see that poetry can be relevant to the modern – and especially younger – reader, and that words have a power, no matter what form they take.

1

According to Alexandra, things are changing, with more young people producing some really fantastic work. Since the time nearly five years ago when Alexandra set up an annual competition for young poets, she has been hugely encouraged by the increase in both the quality and quantity of the poems that are submitted. She is always pleased to see how keen the winners are to share their poems with the audience. Any chance to listen to well-known poets reading their work aloud is a potential opportunity for people to be inspired to get writing, she explains, and perhaps even become successful! Anyone interested in hearing some exciting poets reading their work could even get to listen to Alexandra herself, as she schedules poetry performances in concert halls and theatres. However, they tend to sell out quickly.

Alexandra is always looking for new ways to support young writers, as she remembers how much she would have appreciated advice and guidance when she was starting out. To help her do this, she makes regular appearances at literature festivals around the country, and is always delighted to meet new poets, read their work, and offer feedback – as long as the poet wants that, of course! And the most useful tip she passes on? Well, that would have to be to accept that, sometimes, new ideas don’t come. It’s fine, she says, and goes on to admit that it’s something that happens to even experienced poets!
